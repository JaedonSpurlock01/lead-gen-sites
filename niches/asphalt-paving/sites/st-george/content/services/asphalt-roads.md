## Private roads, lanes, and approaches

Asphalt roads for private communities, farm approaches, long residential lanes, and commercial drives need more structure than a backyard driveway. Asphalt Paving St George paves these sections in St. George where owners—not the city—maintain the pavement.

A road fails from the bottom. Subgrade that softens during concentrated storm runoff, shoulders that trap water, and a thin mat under truck traffic will map every weak spot into the surface. We plan around traffic, drainage, and visible soil conditions, then pave only when the base is ready.

This service covers new asphalt roads, widening, and rehabilitation of existing private pavement. If you are matching a public road at an apron, we coordinate the joint so the private section does not settle below the street and hold water.

Private road paving in St. George is asphalt contractor work at road thickness — not a stretched driveway mat — with shoulders and drainage planned for the traffic you describe.

## What you gain with a real section

Residents get a cleaner surface that does not throw rock or dust. Owners get pavement they can budget for, because crack care and a future overlay are possible when the original base was compacted. Gravel lanes feel cheaper until repeated grading and dust control are included.

We keep the scope readable. You will see length, width, approximate square footage, whether we are overlaying or rebuilding, and how water is supposed to leave the roadway. If a culvert or a soft spot changes the plan, we pause and price it before the extra work happens.

- Sections sized for cars, service trucks, or mixed traffic
- Shoulders and slopes that move water off the mat
- Joints at driveways and public road aprons
- A maintenance path of crack fill and future overlay

## How a road project runs

Start with the free estimate. Share the approximate size and tell us whether the road is a lane, a cul-de-sac, or an approach. We visit, measure, and look at ditches, utilities, and places a haul truck can turn around. Tight canyon lots above St. George sometimes need a paving plan that respects grade and sight distance.

Prep may include pulverizing or removing the old surface, correcting soft soils, and placing aggregate in compacted lifts. Hot-mix follows in a thickness that matches traffic, placed and rolled while workable. We watch longitudinal joints on wider roads so heat and water do not open the seam.

When the work is done we walk the section with you, confirm drainage, and review when traffic can return. Heavy equipment should stay off until the mat has cooled and set. Sealcoating is a later maintenance step, not the last pass on pave day.
